About

I'm a final-year architecture student at Oxford Brookes. I design buildings around the people who use them, how they are made and how they feel to move through. Alongside architecture I work toward film and TV set design, where the same skills tell a story through space, light and atmosphere.

I'm looking for opportunities in architectural practice and in film and TV art departments. My strongest project, Set One Studios, brings both together: a fabrication hub in East London where sets, props and costumes are built for the studios next door.

Year 2Unit L: Making Housing Public+

One estate, three projects, each bigger than the last. The Malmesbury Estate in Bow is a postwar estate in decline: amenities have closed, the blocks are falling into disrepair and the community has little say. Each project gives residents a way to make, repair and earn for themselves.

  1. 01 / RepairOffset StudiosA rooftop workshop, up to 16 m²
  2. 02 / ExchangeCommon Ground StudiosWorkshop and assembly hall
  3. 03 / ProductionSet One StudiosFilm fabrication hub and estate upgrade

Oxford Brookes, Unit L, 2025 to 26. Tutors: Mark Rist, Natalie Savva, Toby Dauncey

Unit L, Part 1: Repair

Offset Studios

A small repair and craft workshop bolted onto the roof of an existing block. Residents come to fix things, learn skills from each other and make. The idea started at the V&A East Storehouse, just up the road from Bow, where collections sit in tall containers that visitors can look into. Offset borrows that: a display wall where residents' work is stored in full view, turning the estate's own craft into its own collection.

Drawings and model photos being added

Unit L, Part 2: Exchange

Common Ground Studios

Common Ground scales Offset's idea up into a permanent building. Residents make and repair furniture in the workshop, then bring it into the assembly hall, a room that works as both auction house and town hall.

Auctions put Bow's craft on the map and bring money in. On other nights the same room is where the community meets to fix its own problems. A glass-brick chimney wraps the spiral stair and glows at night, a nod to the industrial stacks that once marked Bow's skyline.

02

How it is built

An A0 sectional perspective at 1:20, cut through the workshop, the cantilevered first floor and the chimney stair. Open it full screen and zoom in to read every layer.

Technical decisions

  1. Reclaimed steel cantilever. Reclaimed 254x146 UB beams carry the first floor out over the workshop, cutting embodied carbon and nodding to warehouse construction. Intumescent paint takes them to R60.
  2. The chimney stair. A central steel cylinder carries the stair and the aluminium chimney above. Glass brick replaces solid brick so filtered light gets in and the stair reads from the street.
  3. Breathable walls. STEICO wood fibre insulation, lime plaster and a ventilated brick rainscreen keep the wall vapour-open, with no risk of condensation inside it.
  4. Sun and air. East glazing warms the ground floor slab before the workshop opens. High roof openings pull cool air in through the workshop door: passive stack ventilation.
  5. Water. A box gutter hidden in the corrugated steel roof feeds underground storage for toilet flushing, tool cleaning and dust suppression.

Unit L, Part 3: Production

Set One Studios

A fabrication hub for film and TV on the Malmesbury Estate in Bow, a short walk from Three Mills Studios. Sets, props and costumes are built here, and part of every pound earned goes back into upgrading the estate around it.

A three-storey hempcrete and timber workshop inside a polycarbonate shell. A skybridge links it to the existing block, which gains a workers' café, a CAD studio, winter gardens and new community rooms.

01

Site and story

Set One started with Bow's industrial past. Researching the old works and the chimney stacks that still mark the skyline led to Three Mills Studios, a working film studio just down the River Lea. With London's film industry growing, Set One gives it something Bow can build.

Physical site model

02

Studying sets

I drew over film stills to understand how a set works: a constructed world that looks real from one side and shows its workings from the other. The notes pick out the artificial world behind the real one, and the diagram places Set One where home, play and work overlap.

03

Plans

The new building holds a materials depot, a textiles depot, the main workshop, a smaller tool workshop, a textiles workshop and a rooftop inside the shell. The bridge carries a CAD studio and the workers' café into the upgraded block.

04

Built like a set

Set One turns a Lacaton & Vassal idea inside out. Rather than wrapping a winter garden around a building, a solid hempcrete and timber workshop sits inside a steel frame clad in polycarbonate. The gap between the two is the circulation, so moving through the building feels like walking backstage.

Technical decisions

  1. A warm buffer. The 32 mm polycarbonate shell traps solar heat in a 1,861 mm cavity. With the 300 mm hempcrete wall behind it, it works like a Trombe wall, storing heat and releasing it slowly.
  2. Air on demand. Operable polycarbonate panels and high and low windows drive stack ventilation through the full height of the workshop.
  3. Fire. Exposed timber beams and columns are treated with intumescent paint for 60 minutes. Hempcrete infill is non-combustible and needs no extra protection.
  4. The shell frame. 40x25 mm RHS beams at 1,775 mm centres hold the polycarbonate, sized for Southeast London wind loads.
  5. Overexpression. An oversized western red cedar lintel sits proud of the wall on purpose, a sign of the woodworking going on inside.
  6. Water. A concealed gutter feeds a greywater system for the site's bathrooms and WCs.

Built like a set

The Set Design Court

The rooftop courts take the pitched and sawtooth rooflines of the surrounding streets and flatten them into 2D panels on perforated steel posts.

They come down as easily as they go up, and residents choose which sports are played. The same logic as a flat on a sound stage.

05

Inside

Warm, heavy timber and hempcrete inside; light, glowing polycarbonate outside. Every route between them passes through the gap.

06

Giving back to the estate

Tutor crits pushed the project to tackle the wider problems facing social housing. So Set One became the engine of an estate upgrade: part of its income flows into a Bow Upgrade Fund, and its scrap timber fuels a biomass burner for the block next door.

For residents

  • Reception, post room, bike store and a new lift
  • Balconies and winter gardens
  • Wash room, library and study room
  • Green space and an outdoor cinema
  • Squash court and the Set Design Court on the roof
  • Solar power, a biomass burner and rainwater for flushing

Digital Culture module

Light studies

An everyday IKEA lamp, measured by hand, then rebuilt digitally piece by piece: modelled, drawn and rendered to test how light, material and form read on screen. A study in looking closely at an object and making it believable, the same skill a prop drawing needs.
